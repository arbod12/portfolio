Positions — names and jobs

QBQuarterback

Takes the snap, reads the defense, hands off or throws.

RB / HBRunning back (halfback)

Lines up in the backfield; runs, catches, and helps block.

FBFullback

A bigger back who usually lead-blocks for the RB.

WR — XSplit end

Receiver on the line of scrimmage, usually the widest on his side.

WR — ZFlanker

Receiver a step off the line, so he can go in motion.

SlotSlot receiver

Lines up between the outside receiver and the line.

TE — YTight end

Lines up next to a tackle; blocks like a lineman or runs routes.

LT · LG · C · RG · RTOffensive line

Left tackle, left guard, center, right guard, right tackle. The center snaps the ball.

DE / EDGEDefensive end

Rushes from the outside edge of the line; sets the edge against runs.

DT / NTDefensive tackle / nose tackle

Inside linemen. A nose tackle lines up right over the center.

MIKE · WILL · SAMLinebackers

Middle, weak-side and strong-side linebackers. They stop runs, cover, and blitz.

CBCornerback

Covers wide receivers. Press = right on the receiver at the snap; off = a few yards back.

NBNickel back

A fifth defensive back who usually covers the slot receiver.

FS · SSFree and strong safety

The last line of defense. The free safety plays deep; the strong safety often plays closer to the line.

11, 12, 21…Personnel groupings

First digit = running backs, second = tight ends. 11 = 1 RB, 1 TE, so 3 WR.

Coverage shells and routes

Offense in navy, defense in white. Shaded areas are zones; dashed orange arrows are man-to-man assignments; red arrows are blitzers.

Field: 120 × 53⅓ yards, 10-yard end zones, hash marks 70 ft 9 in from each sideline (18 ft 6 in apart, the same width as the goalposts), numbers 6 ft tall with their tops 9 yards from the sideline.

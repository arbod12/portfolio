Positions — names and jobs

CCenter

Takes faceoffs, plays both ends, usually the deepest forward in his own zone.

LW · RWLeft and right wing

Forwards who work their side of the ice: boards, corners and the net front.

LD · RDLeft and right defense

Defensemen. Hold the blue line on offense, protect the slot and net front on defense.

GGoalie

Plays the angles. “Five-hole” is the gap between the pads.

F1 · F2 · F3Forechecking roles

First forward in pressures the puck; F2 supports; F3 stays high as safety.

PointBlue-line spots

Where defensemen stand in the offensive zone, feeding shots and keeping the puck in.

Half-wallBoards between point and corner

Where power plays often run their offense.

Slot / home plateThe scoring area

Between the faceoff dots in front of the net, up to the top of the circles.

PP · PKPower play · penalty kill

5 vs. 4 (or 5 vs. 3) after a penalty. Overtime here was 4-on-4.

Offside · icingTwo basic rules

You can’t enter the zone ahead of the puck; you can’t fire it from your half past the far goal line untouched (with exceptions).

Zones, forechecks and special teams

Attacking team in red (skating up the screen), defending team in white. Shaded areas are areas of the ice; lines show where players move.

Rink: 200 × 85 ft with 28-ft corner radius; goal lines 11 ft from the end boards; blue lines 12 in wide, with 64 ft from each goal line to the far edge of its blue line, leaving a 50-ft neutral zone (11 + 64 + 50 + 64 + 11 = 200 ft); neutral-zone faceoff spots 5 ft outside each blue line; faceoff circles 15-ft radius with dots 20 ft from the goal line and 22 ft either side of center; goal 6 ft wide; crease a 6-ft arc cut off 1 ft outside each post; trapezoid 18 ft wide at the goal line and 28 ft at the end boards. Plays are reconstructed from broadcast footage and published accounts; spots and timing are close approximations, not tracking data.
